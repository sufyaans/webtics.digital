Ask five agencies what a website costs and you will get five very different answers. That is not because anyone is being dishonest. It is because "a website" can mean anything from a one-page template to a custom-built sales tool.

This guide explains what actually drives the price, so you can compare quotes properly and spend what makes sense for your business.

## What drives the cost of a website

### 1. How many unique page designs you need

A five-page site where every page shares one layout is quicker to build than a site with a homepage, service pages, case studies and a blog, each with its own design. Count the *types* of page, not just the pages.

### 2. Who writes the content

Good copy is the difference between a site that looks nice and one that brings in enquiries. If the agency writes it, plans it around search terms and edits it with you, that is real work and it belongs in the quote.

### 3. Custom design or a template

A theme is cheaper upfront. A custom design is built around your customers and your brand. The real question is whether looking like everyone else is costing you work.

### 4. Integrations and tracking

Booking tools, CRMs, payment, WhatsApp, analytics with proper conversion tracking. Each one adds time, and each one is usually worth it.

## The hidden costs nobody mentions

- **Plugin licences and updates.** Plugin-heavy sites need constant patching. Skipped updates are the most common way small business sites get hacked.
- **Hosting that is not really hosting.** Cheap shared hosting is often slow, and a slow site ranks and converts worse.
- **Being locked in.** Some agencies keep ownership of the site or the domain. Always check.
- **Changes after launch.** If every small edit needs a new quote, those costs add up.

<Callout title="A quick test for any quote">
  Ask: Who owns the website and domain? Where is it hosted? How are updates and security handled? How will I know whether it is working? Clear answers are a good sign.
</Callout>
