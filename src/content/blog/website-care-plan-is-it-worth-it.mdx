A website is not a one-off purchase. Like a van or a shop front, it needs looking after. A care plan is simply an agreement that someone will do that for you.

## What a good care plan includes

- **Hosting** that is fast, secure and backed up.
- **Monitoring** for downtime, speed and broken forms, with alerts when something goes wrong.
- **Security**: updates, HTTPS, security headers and a plan if anything is compromised.
- **Small changes**: new text, images, opening hours, a new team member.
- **A monthly report** that shows visits, enquiries and anything that was fixed.

## When a care plan is worth it

### Your website brings in work

If a broken contact form could cost you a week of enquiries, monitoring alone is worth paying for. Most forms fail silently. Nobody notices until someone mentions they never heard back.

### Your site has been hacked before

Sites that have been compromised are often targeted again. A care plan should include hardening, monitoring and a clear recovery plan.

<PullQuote>
  Most broken contact forms are discovered by an annoyed customer, not by the business owner.
</PullQuote>

### Nobody owns the job

If updates, renewals and backups are "someone's job" but not anyone's in particular, they will not happen.

## When it is not worth it

If your site is a simple, static page that rarely changes and does not bring in leads, you may not need much more than reliable hosting. A good agency will tell you that.

<Callout title="Questions to ask any provider">
  What exactly is monitored? How quickly do you respond when something breaks? How many hours of changes are included? Do I get a report? What happens to my site if I leave?
</Callout>
