Power BI is often sold as something for big companies with data teams. In reality, some of the best uses are in small businesses, where one person is spending hours every week stitching numbers together by hand.

## What a dashboard actually is

A dashboard is a single screen that answers the questions you ask most often, using data that refreshes itself. That is all. No more copying figures into a spreadsheet on a Friday afternoon.

## Five questions a small business dashboard can answer

1. **How are sales tracking against last month and last year?**
2. **Which products or services make the most profit, not just the most revenue?**
3. **Where do new customers come from?** Website, referrals, social or repeat business.
4. **Which customers have gone quiet?**
5. **Is the website bringing in enquiries, and from which pages?**

<PullQuote>
  Start with the questions you ask on a Monday morning. The charts come later.
</PullQuote>

## Where the data comes from

Most small businesses already have everything they need:

- Spreadsheets and shared drives
- Accounting software
- Tills, booking systems or e-commerce platforms
- A CRM or even just an email inbox
- Google Analytics and Search Console for the website

Power BI can connect to most of these directly. Where it cannot, a small automated step usually bridges the gap.

<Callout title="Do I need clean data first?">
  No. Cleaning and organising the data is part of the job. In practice, it is often where a lot of the value is found, because inconsistencies show up quickly once everything is in one place.
</Callout>
