## The challenge

Trans Maritime's previous website dated back to 2009. It had been built and never really touched since, and over the years it had been hacked more than once. By the time we were brought in, Google had blacklisted the domain as unsafe. For a freight forwarder whose customers are often trusting them with cargo worth far more than the website itself, a browser warning instead of a home page is about as bad as a first impression gets.

The business also moves cargo by sea, air, road and rail, and handles warehousing, customs clearance and project cargo on top of that. That is a lot to explain clearly, on a site that also had to feel dependable rather than dated.

## Our approach

Patching the old site was never going to be enough. We rebuilt it from the ground up on a clean domain reputation, so the blacklist and the years of accumulated vulnerabilities were left behind with the old code rather than carried forward.

We organised the new site around what a customer is trying to ship and how. Each solution has its own page, reached from one simple menu, so nobody has to dig for the service they need. The visual design moved on from the 2009 original to a modern, confident look that matches a company moving cargo internationally in 2024, not one still running on technology from a decade and a half earlier.

## The solution

- Rebuilt on WordPress with Elementor, hardened and launched on a clean domain reputation, away from the blacklisted history of the old site.
- A bold home page that says what the company does in one line.
- A dedicated page for each solution: sea, air and road freight, warehousing and distribution, customs clearance, the inland container depot, consolidation and project cargo.
- An FAQ and a short contact form, with click-to-call and email links throughout.
- A layout that works as well on a phone as on a desktop.

## Results

The clearest result here is not a chart. It is what visitors no longer see: a security warning in place of a freight company's home page. The 2009 site that Google had blacklisted has been fully retired, and Trans Maritime now has a modern site built to be maintained rather than left to rot for another fifteen years.
